# Acuting OS Parallel Clinical Curriculum and Future Practice Strategy

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Purpose:** Capture the learning and professional-development strategy discussed after the original Acuting OS vision and governance document.  
**Scope:** Bastyr doctorate completion, external knowledge supplementation, North American acupuncture core practice, healthy aging, integrative medicine, and clinical innovation.

---

# 1. Strategic Goal

The goal is not to become a practitioner who knows a little about everything.

The goal is to graduate with a coherent and defensible professional identity:

> **An evidence-informed acupuncture and Chinese medicine practitioner focused on pain, functional aging, women’s health, and integrative symptom care, supported by clinical informatics and AI.**

Chinese summary:

> **以疼痛、功能老化、女性健康與整合症狀照護為核心，具備循證醫學、臨床安全與醫療資訊系統能力的中醫針灸師。**

The Bastyr doctorate should serve as:

- The primary U.S. professional credential
- The clinical training base
- The licensure pathway
- The foundation for supervised patient care
- The platform for learning U.S. healthcare communication and documentation

However, no single school should be expected to provide deep mastery in all of the following:

- Classical Chinese medicine
- Acupuncture technique
- Herbal medicine
- Western diagnosis
- Pharmacology
- Geriatrics
- Pain science
- Neurorehabilitation
- Women’s health
- Oncology support
- Research methods
- Evidence appraisal
- Clinical informatics
- AI-assisted medical knowledge systems

Therefore, the correct long-term model is a:

> **Parallel Clinical Curriculum**

This means completing the Bastyr doctorate while deliberately building additional modules from stronger external institutions, research centers, mentors, hospitals, and structured self-study.

---

# 2. Core Learning Philosophy

## 2.1 Do Not Duplicate the Same Degree

After completing the Bastyr doctorate, another nearly identical U.S. acupuncture doctorate is unlikely to provide enough additional value.

External education should fill specific gaps rather than repeat the same general curriculum.

Priority gap areas:

- Biomedical diagnosis
- Differential diagnosis
- Red flags and referral
- Pharmacology
- Herb-drug interaction
- Research literacy
- Clinical outcomes
- Rehabilitation
- Healthy aging
- Digital health
- Clinical informatics
- Integrative oncology
- Evidence-based integrative medicine

## 2.2 Build Modules, Not Credentials for Their Own Sake

Preferred format:

- Short clinical intensives
- Hospital observation
- Continuing education
- Certificate programs
- Research-method courses
- Mentorship
- Focused specialty training
- Structured online learning
- Small research or informatics projects

A new degree is only justified if it adds a genuinely different second pillar, such as:

- Clinical research
- Epidemiology
- Health informatics
- Rehabilitation science
- Pain science
- Gerontology
- Public health

## 2.3 Use External Material to Design the Framework

Senior notes, older integrated notes, syllabi, textbooks, and external school curricula should be used to discover:

- What subjects will appear later
- Which entity types Acuting OS must support
- Which relationships will be needed
- Which areas are time-sensitive
- Which gaps are not covered well by the current school

Older notes are useful as a **domain map**, not as current clinical authority.

# 4. Essential Knowledge Foundation

Before specialization, the following areas should be strong enough for safe independent practice.

## 4.1 Biomedical Diagnosis

Must understand:

- Common disease presentations
- Differential diagnosis
- Basic laboratory interpretation
- Basic imaging concepts
- When symptoms exceed acupuncture scope
- When conventional diagnosis changes treatment safety

## 4.2 Red Flags and Referral

Must identify and respond appropriately to:

- Stroke or TIA symptoms
- Acute coronary symptoms
- Cauda equina syndrome
- Progressive neurologic deficit
- Fracture
- Infection
- Cancer warning signs
- Severe anemia
- Delirium
- Suicidality
- Abnormal bleeding
- Medication toxicity
- Acute abdominal or pelvic emergencies

## 4.3 Pharmacology

Must understand common:

- Anticoagulants
- Antiplatelet agents
- Antihypertensives
- Diabetes medications
- Analgesics
- NSAIDs
- Opioids
- Antidepressants
- Sedatives
- Hormonal therapies
- Oncology medications
- Neurologic medications

## 4.4 Herb-Drug Interactions

Must distinguish:

- Pharmacokinetic interaction
- Pharmacodynamic interaction
- Additive bleeding risk
- Sedation
- Hepatotoxicity
- Nephrotoxicity
- QT-prolongation concern
- CYP-related interaction
- Documented clinical interaction
- Theoretical interaction
- Unknown or conflicting evidence

## 4.5 Evidence Appraisal

Must be able to interpret:

- Clinical guidelines
- Systematic reviews
- Meta-analyses
- RCTs
- Observational studies
- Mechanism studies
- Animal studies
- Risk of bias
- Clinical versus statistical significance
- Applicability to the actual patient

## 4.6 Clinical Documentation and Outcomes

Must be able to document:

- Functional complaint
- Relevant history
- Examination
- Safety screening
- Treatment goal
- Intervention
- Response
- Follow-up
- Referral
- Coordination with other clinicians

# 5. North American Acupuncture Core

These are the areas that should remain central because they are common, clinically relevant, understandable to other healthcare professionals, and generally aligned with the strongest acupuncture demand in North America.

---

## 5.1 Chronic Pain and Musculoskeletal Care

Priority conditions:

- Chronic low back pain
- Neck pain
- Knee osteoarthritis
- Shoulder pain
- Myofascial pain
- Hip pain
- Ankle and foot pain
- Postural pain
- Movement-related pain
- Selected postoperative pain

Required competencies:

- Basic orthopedic and neurologic screening
- Distinguish joint, nerve, muscle, fascia, and referred pain
- Recognize red flags
- Movement and gait observation
- Functional goal setting
- Outcome tracking
- Referral to physical therapy, orthopedics, imaging, or emergency care when appropriate

Professional differentiation:

> **Acupuncture + fascia + structural assessment + movement + pain neuroscience + rehabilitation**

Do not reduce the practice to “fascia” alone. The clinician must still understand disease, neurologic findings, functional impairment, and referral thresholds.

---

## 5.2 Headache and Migraine

Priority conditions:

- Migraine with aura
- Migraine without aura
- Tension-type headache
- Cervicogenic headache
- Medication-overuse headache
- TMJ-associated headache
- Hormone-related headache patterns

Required competencies:

- Secondary headache red flags
- Basic neurologic screening
- Cervical and jaw assessment
- Trigger identification
- Medication awareness
- Outcome measures
- Clear communication about realistic treatment goals

---

## 5.3 Osteoarthritis, Mobility, and Functional Maintenance

Priority issues:

- Knee osteoarthritis
- Hip stiffness
- Chronic joint pain
- Mobility decline
- Fear of falling
- Reduced activity tolerance
- Balance problems
- Gait changes

Important outcome goals:

- Improved walking
- Improved function
- Increased activity
- Better sleep
- Reduced fear
- Reduced pain interference
- Maintenance of independence

---

## 5.4 Common Adjunctive Symptom Care

These may be common in practice, but evidence strength must be displayed honestly and condition by condition.

Examples:

- Insomnia
- Anxiety
- Stress-related symptoms
- Menopausal symptoms
- Dysmenorrhea
- Chronic pelvic pain
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Fatigue
- Functional digestive complaints
- Constipation

Acuting OS must distinguish:

```text
Commonly treated
≠ Strong evidence
≠ Appropriate for every patient
≠ Insurance covered
```

# 6. Healthy Aging and Functional Longevity

The preferred framing is:

> **Healthy Aging and Functional Longevity**

Avoid building a practice around unverified “reverse aging” claims.

The goal is to preserve:

- Mobility
- Cognition
- Independence
- Sleep
- Appetite
- Emotional health
- Social participation
- Ability to perform daily activities
- Quality of life
- Healthspan rather than marketing-based lifespan claims

---

- Basic gait observation
- Balance screening
- Fall history
- Frailty indicators
- Muscle weakness
- Activity tolerance
- Fear of falling

- Normal aging

# 10. Graduation Competency Model

---

## 10.1 Must Be Able to Perform Independently

- Common pain and musculoskeletal evaluation
- Basic headache classification
- TCM pattern differentiation
- Safe needling
- Treatment planning
- Common medication recognition
- Basic herb-drug interaction screening
- Red-flag screening
- Referral decisions
- Reasonable treatment-course planning
- Outcome measurement
- Patient education
- Explanation of evidence limitations

---

## 10.2 Must Know When Not to Treat Independently

- Acute neurologic event
- Acute cardiac symptoms
- Severe infection
- Fracture
- Cancer warning signs
- Severe anemia
- Progressive neurologic deficit
- Delirium
- Suicidality
- Abnormal bleeding
- Acute pelvic or abdominal emergency
- Medication toxicity
- Unstable medically complex patient

---

## 10.3 Must Communicate With Other Healthcare Professionals

Documentation should include:

- Functional complaint
- Relevant biomedical history
- Relevant examination
- Safety screening
- Treatment goal
- Intervention
- Response
- Follow-up
- Referral
- Coordination plan

Do not document only:

```text
Liver Qi stagnation. Treated with LR3.
```

The TCM assessment can remain, but it must coexist with clinically useful communication.

# 13. Use of Senior Notes and Legacy Materials

Existing older integrated senior notes should be used as:

- Curriculum map
- Domain inventory
- Entity discovery tool
- Relationship discovery tool
- Schema stress test
- Gap-analysis source

They should not automatically be treated as:

- Current guidelines
- Verified pharmacology
- Current diagnostic criteria
- Current safety standards
- Current evidence summaries

Recommended status:

```yaml
source_type: legacy_student_notes
framework_use: allowed
clinical_authority: low
verification_status: unverified
freshness_status: outdated_or_unknown
```

Newer senior notes, official syllabi, current slides, textbooks, guidelines, and papers should be used to update time-sensitive content.

# 14. Criteria for Choosing External Training

Before paying for any program, ask:

1. Does it fill a real gap?
2. Is it clinically applicable?
3. Does it improve safety?
4. Does it teach transferable reasoning?
5. Does it provide supervised clinical exposure?
6. Is it evidence-aware?
7. Does it add something Bastyr does not?
8. Can it connect to Acuting OS?
9. Is the certificate actually meaningful?
10. Would the same result be possible through mentorship or CE at lower cost?

Pain and rehabilitation

# 16. Definition of Success at Graduation

Success does not mean knowing every disease.

Success means being able to:

- Evaluate common North American acupuncture presentations
- Treat core conditions safely
- Recognize dangerous conditions
- Communicate with patients and clinicians
- Use herbs responsibly
- Understand common medications
- Track outcomes
- Read evidence
- Refer appropriately
- Care for older adults with realistic goals
- Build structured clinical knowledge
- Continue updating practice after graduation
- Innovate without abandoning safety or professional credibility

The final goal is:

> **A practitioner with a strong clinical foundation, a clear specialty direction, and the ability to create the next generation of integrative medicine tools.**
