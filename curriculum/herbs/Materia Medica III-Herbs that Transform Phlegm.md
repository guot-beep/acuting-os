Materia Medica III-Herbs that Transform Phlegm
Overview
Phlegm-transforming herbs resolve phlegm (tan), redirect rebellious Lung and Stomach qi,
relieve cough and wheezing, and soften nodules or masses. Phlegm is both a pathological
product and a pathogenic factor, frequently complicating chronic disease.
These herbs are subdivided clinically into: - Cold phlegm vs. hot phlegm - Wind-phlegm
obstructing the channels - Phlegm nodules and masses
General Characteristics
Attribute Description
Taste Bitter, acrid
Temperature Warm (cold phlegm) or cold (hot phlegm)
Channels Lung, Spleen (primary)
Core Actions Transform phlegm; stop cough; redirect qi
GLOBAL FUNCTION
Used when phlegm presents as:
• cough/wheeze
• nausea from phlegm obstruction
• nodules, goiter, scrofula
• wind-phlegm stroke patterns
• phlegm-heat agitation
GLOBAL CAUTIONS
• Avoid in Yin deficiency dryness (warm drying herbs)
• Toxic herbs must be processed (Ban Xia, Tian Nan Xing)
• Use cold phlegm herbs cautiously in cold-deficiency patients
• Do not use during the early stages of measles or rashes.
• Stimulating phlegm herbs may worsen bleeding (e.g., hemoptysis).
