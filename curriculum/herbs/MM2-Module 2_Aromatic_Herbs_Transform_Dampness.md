Aromatic Herbs that Transform Dampness
• Aromatic and fragrant
• Enhance the function of the Spleen to dissolve, dry, and disperse dampness.
• Transform and transport substances that would otherwise accumulate to cause
dampness
• Differential diagnosis: Damp-cold, damp-heat, etc.; Combine with other herbs.
• Long-term use of warming and drying herbs may consume yin and body fluids.
• Most of these herbs are aromatic and contain large percentages of essential oils.
They should be added toward the end of the decoction.
• Beneficial to use with herbs that simultaneously activate qi.

Note: Cang Zhu is Yao Yao for dampness obstructing the middle jiao.

Indications – for damp heat pouring downward (as in leg qi), vaginal discharge and swollen,

Actions: Improves vision
Indications – for night blindness, diminished vision, cataracts, and glaucoma.

• Dries dampness, strengthens the Spleen

Hòu Pò 厚 朴
• Cortex Magnoliae Officinalis
• English: magnolia bark
• Properties: bitter, acrid, warm, aromatic
• Channels: Large Intestine, Lung, Spleen, Stomach
Actions and Indications
Actions: Promotes the movement of qi, transforms damp, and resolves stagnation.
Indications – dampness distressing the middle jiao, qi stagnation of the Spleen and
Stomach, and food stagnation.
Note: Hou Po is the key herb (Yao Yao) for treating qi stagnation of the Spleen and Stomach,
leading to epigastric and abdominal fullness and distention, loss of appetite, and poor
digestion.
• Dampness obstructing the middle jiao: Cang Zhu, Chen Pi (Formula: ping wei san )
• Food stagnation with constipation: Da Huang, Zhi Shi (Formula: da xiao cheng qi
tang )
• Qi stagnation with cold: Gui Zhi, Fu Zi, Gan Jiang
• Qi stagnation with deficient cold: Ren Shen, Gan Cao, Sheng Jiang
Actions: Warms and transforms phlegm and directs rebellious qi downward.
Indications – for wheezing, primarily due to phlegm obstruction, with cough and a stifling
sensation in the chest.
