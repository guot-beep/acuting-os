Overview
Wind-extinguishing herbs calm Liver wind, anchor ascendant yang, clear Liver heat, and
open the channels. They are used for dizziness, vertigo, tremors, convulsions, seizures,
stroke sequelae, and headaches related to Liver wind, Liver yang rising, or Liver fire.
Accurate differentiation between excess vs. deficiency is exam-critical.
General Characteristics (High-Yield)
Attribute Description
Taste Salty (often bitter/acrid secondary)
Temperature Cool to cold (some neutral/warm)
Channels Liver (primary)
Core Actions Extinguish wind; anchor yang; clear heat
Global Cautions (Exam-Critical)
• Use cautiously with Spleen/Stomach deficiency and cold.
• Many substances are animal or mineral → sourcing, toxicity, and preparation
matter.
• Distinguish excess heat wind vs. deficiency wind before selection.

Líng Yáng Jiǎo 羚羊角
Cornu Saigae Tataricae | Saiga antelope horn (often substituted)
Nature & Flavor: Salty, cold
Channels: Liver, Heart
Primary Actions
1. Strongly extinguishes Liver wind; stops spasms
2. Clears Liver heat; cools blood
3. Brightens eyes
4. Anchors Liver Yang
5. Clears heat toxin
Key Indications
• High fever with convulsions
• Acute Liver wind from extreme heat
• Childhood seizures or spasms
• Severe Liver fire with headache and red eyes
• Warm febrile disease with delirium or mania
Cautions/Contraindications
• Rare and protected animal product (often substituted clinically)
• Use cautiously in deficiency conditions
Preparation
• Cook separately, then combine with decoction.
Dosage
• Decoction: 1–3 g
• Powder: 0.3–0.5 g
Exam Pearl
Ling Yang Jiao is the strongest wind-extinguisher, making it the Best herb for Liver wind from
extreme heat.
