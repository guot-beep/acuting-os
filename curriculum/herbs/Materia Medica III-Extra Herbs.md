Ling Zhi (灵芝)
Herbal Category
Herbs that Tonify Qi / Calm the Shen
Latin Name | Common Name
Ganoderma lucidum | Reishi mushroom
Nature & Flavor: Sweet, neutral
Channels Entered: Heart, Lung, Liver
Primary Actions
1. Tonifies Qi and nourishes Blood
2. Calms the Shen
3. Stops cough and wheezing
Key Indications
• Insomnia, palpitations, anxiety from Heart deficiency
• Fatigue and weakness due to Qi deficiency
• Chronic cough or wheezing due to Lung deficiency
• Deficiency-type emotional disturbance
Preparation
Decoction, powder, or medicinal mushroom extract
Dosage
6–12 g in decoction
Cautions / Contraindications
• Generally very safe
• Use caution with anticoagulant therapy
Exam Pearl
One of the few herbs that simultaneously tonifies Qi and calms the Shen.

Guan Mu Tong (关木通)
Herbal Category
Herbs that Drain Dampness / Promote Urination
Latin Name | Common Name
Aristolochia manshuriensis | Aristolochia stem
Nature & Flavor: Bitter, cold
Channels Entered: Heart, Small Intestine, Urinary Bladder
Primary Actions
1. Promotes urination
2. Drains Heart heat
3. Promotes lactation
Key Indications
• Lin syndrome
• Heart heat with irritability and mouth sores
• Lactation deficiency
Preparation
Decoction
Dosage
3–6 g
Cautions / Contraindications
• Nephrotoxic (aristolochic acid)
• Contraindicated in kidney disease
• Avoid pregnancy
Exam Pearl
Rarely used clinically today due to aristolochic acid toxicity.
Clinical Insights
Modern practice generally substitutes Chuan Mu Tong or Tong Cao for Guan Mu Tong.
