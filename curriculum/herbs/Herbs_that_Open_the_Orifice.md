Be sure to obtain the correct differential diagnoses, then combine them with other herbs
to achieve the optimal effect.
These herbs are naturally acrid and aromatic. They consume qi and are used only in acute
situations. While they restore consciousness and relieve symptoms, they do not address
the underlying conditions that cause them. They are not used frequently or extensively,
and are primarily used in powder or pill form.

Actions: Vigorously opens the orifices, revives the spirit, and unblocks Bi Zheng
( Closed Disorder)
Indications: impaired consciousness caused by heat and phlegm, stroke, and convulsion.

    � She Xiang is acrid and fragrantly aromatic in nature, that is, it travels through and
         pierces; it is one of the strongest substances in reviving the shen (spirit).

    � As "Yao Yao" it is the key, indispensable ingredient when treating diseases
         accompanied by impaired consciousness.

    � Although it is warm, it treats both hot and cold types of Bi Zheng (closed disorder)
         when combined with other appropriate herbs.

    � Impaired consciousness caused by heat and phlegm, stroke, convulsions, and /or
         heat stroke: Bing Pian, Niu Huang, Zhu Sha (An Gong Niu Huang Wang)

    � Infantile convulsions: Niu Huang, Ling Yang Jiao, Tian Nan Xing, Zhu Sha
    � Impaired consciousness due to cold and phlegm: Su He Xiang, Tan Xiang (Su He

         Xiang Wan)

Actions: Invigorates the blood, dissipates clumps/clots and reduces swelling, and
alleviates pain.
Indications: carbuncles, sores, hot swellings, amenorrhea, and immobile palpable masses.

    � She Xiang is usually applied topically when there is not yet an open lesion on the
         skin. This substance is aromatic and penetrating, and is most suitable for
         dispersing stagnation at the blood level.

    � Carbuncles, sores, and hot swellings: She Xiang, Ru Xiang, Mo Yao
    � Immobile palpable masses: Xhi Shao, San Leng, E Zhu
    � Amenorrhea: Dan Shen, Chi Shao, Hong Hua

Actions: Relieves Pain
Indications: Chest pain (Dan Shen, Tao Ren, Xie Bai ), traumatic injuries with blood
stagnation (She Xiang, Ru Xiang, Yan Hu Suo ( Qi Li San)

Actions: Induces labor in a stillbirth
Indication: stillbirth. With its strong penetrating properties, She Xiang expels a dead
fetus or a placenta that fails to descend.

Main Actions:
    � Vigorously opens the orifices, revives the spirit, and unblocks Bi Zheng (Closed
         Disorder)
    � Invigorates blood, dissipates clumps/clots, reduces swelling, and relieves pain
    � Induces labor if there is a stillbirth

Dosages:
60 to 100 mg as powder or pills. The maximum dosage of Shen Xiang is 1.0 gram.
