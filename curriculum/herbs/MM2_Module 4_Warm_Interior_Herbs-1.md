CCaauuttiioonnss
• Fu Zi is contraindicated during pregnancy.
• It is contraindicated in patients with yang excess and yin deficiency (also
referred to as false cold and true heat).
• Fu Zi is incompatible with Bei Mu, Ban Xia, Gua Lou Shi, Bai Ji, and Bai Lian.
• Consumption of alcohol is contraindicated with Fu Zi, as absorption of the
toxic elements will be significantly enhanced.
NOTES:
Over dosage
Side effects and adverse reactions are usually due to overdose.
Signs and symptoms: involuntary salivation, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness,
blurred vision, dry mouth, vertigo, numbness of the body and extremities, slowed
pulse, difficulty breathing, twitching limbs, convulsions, disorientation, urinary and
fecal incontinence, decreased pressure and body temperature, arrhythmia, and
possible death.
Treatment of overdose
1. Rou Gui is usually used to reverse early-stage overdose within 4 to 6 hours
of the ingestion of Fu Zi. Rou Gui tea was prepared by soaking 5 to 10 grams of the
herbs in hot water. The tea should induce vomiting of the toxin within 5 to 15
minutes, and relieve overall symptoms within 15 to 30 minutes.
2. Overdose of Fu Zi can also be treated with a decoction containing Sheng
Jiang, Gan Jiang, Lu Dou, Hei Dou, Huang Lian, Ren Shen, and Huang Qi
Herb-drug interaction
Patients who have a history of cardiovascular disorders or are taking antiarrhythmic
medications should take Fu Zi with extreme caution.
