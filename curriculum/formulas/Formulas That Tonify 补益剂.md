## p.1

Formulas That Tonify 

� Among the 8 strategies, this belongs to the
  tonifying ().

� Formulas in this chapter are used to treat �
  qi xu, blood xu, qi and blood xu, yin xu, and
  yang xu.

� Between qi and blood, yin and yang,
  tonifying one will have a salutary effect on
  the other.

## p.2

� In "Discussion of the Spleen and Stomach" Li Dong Yuan
   said: "For blood xu, use ren shen to tonify, because when
   the yang qi is exuberant, it will generate yin blood".

� In "Collected Treatises of Jing-Yue" Zhang Jing Yue said:
   "To tonify the yang well, you must search for yang within
   the yin... To tonify the yin well, you must search for yin
   within the yang."

� Many formulas that tonify the yang(qi) therefore contain
   ingredients which tonify the yin(blood), and vice versa.

� For deficiency, tonify the mother is often applied as a
   strategy for tonification. E.g. tonify water to tonify wood.

� Side-effects will often develop when tonics are prescribed
   for individuals who are not suffering from xu � fever, HA,
   rash, pimples, insomnia, irritability, and digestive upset.

## p.3

� Too deficient to be tonified  � the rich,
  cloying nature of tonifying herbs places a burden
  on the digestive system. It is usually necessary to
  add ingredients that promote digestion and
  assimilation. E.g. chen pi, sha ren.

� Mild, chronic conditions require a smaller dosage
  than do acute, life-threatening conditions.

� Tonifying formulas are generally cooked for a
  rather long period of time over a low flame
  (between 30'-1hr.), and are taken on an empty
  stomach to realize their full effect.
