## Category-level Source Notes — 補益 / Tonifying

- Chenoweth course p.59 places tonifying among the **8 strategies as Bu Fa 補法** and uses it for Qi, Blood, Qi+Blood, Yin and Yang deficiency.
- The course notes the mutual relationship of Qi/Blood and Yin/Yang and the strategy “for deficiency, tonify the mother.”
- Source cautions that giving tonics to non-deficient patients may produce fever, headache, rash/pimples, insomnia, irritability and digestive upset.
- “Too deficient to be tonified / 虛不受補”: rich/cloying tonics may burden digestion; the course suggests supporting digestion/assimilation (e.g., Chen Pi, Sha Ren), beginning with milder tonics and increasing strength gradually.
- The course states mild chronic conditions generally use smaller doses than acute life-threatening conditions; tonifying formulas are generally cooked **30–60 min over low flame** and taken on an **empty stomach**.
- General course preparation rules elsewhere in the same material: aromatics such as **Mu Xiang / Sha Ren** are added near the end; **E Jiao** is dissolved in the strained decoction; **Ren Shen / Xi Yang Shen** are listed among expensive herbs that can be separately decocted/simmered. These are general rules and are not silently substituted for formula-specific instructions.
