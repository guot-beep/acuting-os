                                             Uterine  Bleeding

Uterine bleeding beyond menstrual period, either copious or continuously spotting, is generally defined as Beng Lou in TCM.

Beng:   Sudden onset and profuse bleeding.
Lou:     Insidious onset and scanty bleeding.

Although one is an acute and the other a chronic condition, they often transform into each other in the process of this conditions, i.e.
Profuse bleeding consumes Qi and blood (Beng), leading to continuous scanty bleeding(Lou);  Prolonged scanty bleeding(Lou) due to Qi deficiency would chang to profuse bleeding(Beng).

Etiology and Pathogenesis

TCM attributes Beng Lou to:

1.   Excess heat
       Heart heat damages Chong & Ren
       Yin xu leads to lack of nourishment of heart and liver, results in xu fire internally
         burning in the Blood sea
2.   Qi deficiency
       Spleen xu unable to control blood flow
       Kidney Qi xu during the puberty or menopause, leads to lack of nourishment of
       Chong & Ren
3.  Chong & Ren damage, failure in controlling Yin blood
      abortion, miscarriage, postpartum and surgery injure Chong & Ren
      wind cold invasion to the uterus, causes blood stagnation and blockage
      emotional stimulations cause liver Qi and blood stagnation, leads to blockage of
       Chong & Ren

Western medicine attributes uterine bleeding to:

Hormone imbalance:  the most common cause. The imbalance is sometimes caused by improper use of hormone medicine
Polyps:  which are growths on the cervix or inside the uterus
Fibroids:  which are noncancerous growths in the uterus
A cyst on the ovary
Use of an IUD (intrauterine device)
Pregnancy complications:  including miscarriage, threat of miscarriage, and ectopic
 pregnancy
Cancer of the uterus or ovary or cervix
Chronic medical disorders:  e.g. blood-clotting problems, diabetes, and thyroid problems

Case Study

A 42 y-o female comes in with heavy menstruation for more than a year.
This woman has been had heavy menstrual flow each month for 10 to 12 days since she turned 40.  She is telling us that lately  she became very stressed out with her kids enter their teenage years and they are rather hard to deal with every day.
She also tells us her periods are extremely heavy and sometimes are likely gushing and hardly runs to bathrooms, especially at the 2nd, 3rd and 4th days.  Then the bleeding is fading but most months keep spotting until next period and gushing for 2 to 3 days again.
She looks very tired, weak, no spirit and pale.  She speaks in a very low voice and sometimes discontinuously (intermittently).  She feels cold, more pronounced at the lower abdomen and the back.  She also complains that she tends to get dizzy, headachy and chilly around the waist area.  We notice she has a deep sighing from time to time, and appears miserable while she is talking.

Diagnosis:
The patient may need a blood test or procedure, such as:

Endometrial biopsy:  a sample of tissue from the inside of the uterus is examined under a
                                   Microscope.
Ultrasound scan:  sound waves are used to get pictures of the uterus, ovaries, and pelvis.
Sonohysterogram:  an ultrasound scan is done after fluid is injected through a tube into
                               the uterus, to look for problems with lining of the uterus, such as
                               fibroids.
Hysteroscope:  a thin metal tube with a light and tiny camera inserted through the vagina
                          and cervix and into the uterus, to look at the inside of the uterus.
Laparoscopy:  a thin metal tube with a light and tiny camera inserted into the abdomen
                         and pelvis through a small cut in or just below the bellybutton, to look at
                         the uterus and other pelvic organs.
D & C (dilation and curettage):  opens up the cervix and scrapes, or suctions tissue
                                                    from the lining of the uterus, then the tissue examined
                                                    in the lab.
Hysterosalpingography:  dye is injected into the uterus and fallopian tubes through the
                                        cervix.  X-rays are then taken.  The dye outlines the shape and
                                        size of the uterus and tubes.

Treatment:
The treatment depends on the cause of uterine bleeding. The patient may be given hormone treatment if it is caused by hormone imbalance.  Sometimes surgery is needed.  Possible surgical treatments include:

D & C:  in which tissue is scraped or suctioned from the uterus
Hysteroscopy:  to remove a polyp, for example
Endometrial ablation:   which is the use of a laser, electrocautery instrument, or thermal
                                      balloon to destroy the inside lining of the uterus.
Hysterectomy,  which is removal of the uterus.
