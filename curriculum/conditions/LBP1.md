Lower Back Pain

Lower back pain (LBP yao tong) is first mentioned in Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic (huang di nei jing,: su wen), Chapter 41.
LBP can occur unilaterally, bilaterally or centrally. LBP is present in many diseases such as those of the kidneys, Ob-gyn, spinal column, and lumbar muscles. TCM holds that the lower back region is the residence of the Kidneys, thus LBP has a close relationship with the Kidneys.

Etiology & pathogenesis

Kidneys, Lr, Du, UB, Dai

Excess (shi) - Contraction of EPI: Invasion of cold-damp or damp-heat
                                               Overexertion & trauma-sprain/contusion
                                          Leading to qi & blood stagnation in the meridians & collaterals
Deficiency(xu) - Constitutional weakness
                       Old age
                       Chronic illness
                                          Leading to exhaustion & weakening of essence/blood, causing  a decline of K essence, which can deprive the meridians & collaterals of nourishment

Differentiation

Key Dx Questions - a detailed case history. Attention to the location & nature of LBP. Its relation to weather changes and exertion. And aggravating factors. Accompanying symptoms & signs.
PE Points - checks on the spinal vertebrae, lumbar muscles, gynecology, blood, urine, and X-ray, CT scan, MRI....
Tenderness can be detected between the spinal processes of the vertebrae, or in the   lumbar muscles bilaterally or unilaterally in patients with injury to the soft tissues due to
overstrain. No apparent tenderness can be detected in patients with hypertrophic spondylitis.

1. LBP located at the spine, accompanied by local tenderness or deformity indicates diseases of the spinal vertebrae. E.g. fracture or dislocation of a vertebrae caused by trauma, herniated intervertebral discs (L4-L5, L5-S1)due to trauma, rheumatoid arthritis, hypertrophic spondylitis, & tuberculosis of the vertebrae.
2. Lateral LBP indicates disorders of the lumbar muscles. Acute onset of severe LBP with restricted movement of the lower back plus a history of trauma, or bruise at the local area indicates acute sprain of the LB. Chronic onset of LB soreness, aggravated on rainy days or following physical work, indicates rheumatism (w-d feng shi) and chronic injury of lumbar muscles.
3. LBP accompanied by painful, frequent & urgent urination, hematuria or fever indicates diseases of the urinary tract. LBP related to menstrual cycles, or accompanied by profuse leukorrhea, indicates gynecological disorders.

TCM types

1.   Cold-damp - A cold LBP with a heavy dragging sensation that is worse when getting up from bed or on  rainy days. Difficulty in moving the back. A white sticky tongue coating, and a deep pulse.
2.    Damp-heat - LBP with a sensation of heat, a bitter taste in the mouth, restlessness, scanty and deep-yellow urine. A yellow sticky tongue coating, and a soft & rapid pulse.
3. Stagnation of blood - LBP which is usually long-standing and sharp or with traumatic history, fixed in location, and worse on pressure. Difficult in moving the back. A purplish tongue or a tongue with purple spots, and a hesitant pulse.
4. Kidney xu - a dull LBP which is sore and weak in nature, weakness of the legs, or comes and goes, aggravation on exertion. Dizziness blurred vision, tinnitus, nocturnal emission. A thin tongue coating, and a thready deep pulse, pronounced at Chi sections.

1. Cold-damp - transform damp, disperse cold, remove obstruction in the meridians, warm meridians, and relieve pain

2. Damp-heat - clear heat, transform damp, remove obstruction in the meridians, and                 relieve pain

                             UB 23, Du 3, UB 40, LI 11, Du 14, St 40

3. Stagnation of blood - move blood, resolve blood stasis, and remove obstruction in the meridians and collaterals

                             UB 23, Du 3, UB 40, Sp 10, UB 17, Ashi xue, Yaotong xue

4. Kidney xu - tonify kidney essence and qi, consolidate LB, and relieve pain. Moxibustion is also recommended.

                              K yang xu: Du 4, Yaoyan
                              K yin xu: UB 52, K 3

Other acupoints: HTJJ xue

Acute Sprain of the LB

History of sprain
LBP unilateral or bilaterally right after the sprain, or a few hours or one day later
Limited range of motion
Pain worse with movement or coughing/sneezing, or radiating along the involved meridians
PE: muscular tightness in the affected area, absence of apparent bruise or swelling, diffuse or localized tenderness, or deeply located pain
Severe contusion should be differentiated with fracture & injury to the internal organs

Tr.Pr. - Relax the muscles & tendons, move qi, remove obstruction from the meridians and collaterals, and relieve pain.
             Du 26, UB 23, Yaotong xue, Ashi xue, UB 40, UB 60
Du 26 is needled first if LBP is at the posterior midline;
Yaotong xue is needled first if LBP is more lateral.
While the needle is in, advise the patient to move the back at all directions, starting gently.
Then the other points are needled, and cupping at the LB may be applied in the sitting or lying position.
