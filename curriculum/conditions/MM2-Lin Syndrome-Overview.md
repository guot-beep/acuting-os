Lin Syndrome (淋证 Lín Zhèng)
Overview
Lin Syndrome refers to any disorder causing painful or difficult urination and is roughly
equivalent to cystitis, urinary tract infection, urinary calculi, prostatitis, or
glomerulonephritis. It reflects an imbalance of Qi transformation in the Bladder and
Kidney, often influenced by Dampness, Heat, Qi stagnation, or Deficiency of Qi and Yin.
According to Maciocia, “the key pathology is obstruction of the water passages by
pathogenic factors—most often Damp-Heat or Damp-Cold—with possible underlying
deficiency of Qi or Yin that fails to regulate urination.”
Organs most involved
• Bladder (UB) – primary site of excess Heat or Damp obstruction.
• Kidneys (KD) – govern water metabolism; deficiency patterns lead to chronic or
recurrent Lin.
• Spleen (SP) – source of Dampness when transport and transformation are impaired.
• Liver (LV) – contributes through Qi stagnation or Heat transmitting to the Lower
Burner.
Maciocia’s Pattern Classifications
1. Heat Lin (Re Lin 热淋)
Pathogenesis: Damp-Heat in the Bladder.
Clinical features: Frequent, urgent, burning urination with dark, scanty urine; possible
lower abdominal pain or fever.
Tongue/Pulse: Red tongue with yellow, greasy coat; rapid, slippery pulse.
Treatment principle: Clear Heat, drain Damp, unblock urination.
Key herbs: Hua Shi, Mu Tong, Che Qian Zi, Zhi Zi, Deng Xin Cao.
2. Stone Lin (Shi Lin 石淋)
Pathogenesis: Damp-Heat condenses in the Lower Burner, producing stones or gravel.
Symptoms: Severe colicky pain radiating to the lower abdomen or lumbar region, sand or
stones in urine, blood-tinged urine.
Tongue/Pulse: Red, thick yellow coat; wiry or rapid pulse.
Treatment principle: Clear Heat, drain Damp, promote urination, expel stones, relieve
pain.

3. Qi Lin (气淋)
Excess type – Liver Qi stagnation obstructing water passages.
• Dysuria worse with emotional stress, flank distension, wiry pulse.
• Tx: Soothe Liver, regulate Qi, relieve pain.
Deficiency type – Spleen Qi deficiency or Qi sinking.
• Chronic, dribbling urination, bearing-down sensation, fatigue, pale tongue.
• Tx: Tonify Qi, raise Yang, secure urination.
4. Blood Lin (Xue Lin 血淋)
Excess type – Blood Heat.
• Burning, painful urination with blood; dark urine, irritability, thirst.
• Tx: Cool Blood, stop bleeding, clear Heat.
Deficiency type – Spleen Qi or Kidney Yin deficiency.
• Light red urine, chronic mild pain, fatigue, dizziness, dry mouth.
• Tx: Nourish Yin, tonify Qi, stop bleeding.
5. Cloudy Lin (Gao Lin 膏淋)
Excess type – Dampness or Damp-Heat in the Lower Burner.
• Cloudy, turbid, milky urine; heaviness, fullness, greasy tongue coat.
• Tx: Clear Heat, separate Turbidity, drain Damp.
Deficiency type – Spleen or Kidney deficiency.
• Chronic, mild cloudiness, fatigue, lower back weakness, seminal loss.
• Tx: Tonify Spleen, strengthen Kidney, secure essence.

6. Fatigue Lin / Exhausted Lin (Lao Lin 劳淋)
Pathogenesis: Chronic Lin due to depletion of Qi, Blood, or Yin.
Symptoms: Dribbling urination, mild ache, exhaustion after urination, weak lumbar/knees,
pale tongue, deep thin pulse.
Tx principle: Tonify Qi and Yin, strengthen Kidney and Spleen, consolidate urination.

Maciocia-style differentiation highlights
• Heat Lin = Full pattern (excess Heat/Damp) — acute.
• Deficiency Lin patterns = chronic, recurrent, post-infection.
• Qi Lin links the Liver and Bladder via Qi mechanics.
• Blood Lin bridges the concept of Heat injuring collaterals with Bleeding.
• Cloudy Lin reflects turbidity — Dampness or Deficiency of Yang transforming fluids.
• Fatigue Lin resembles Kidney Qi/Yin collapse, often in elderly patients.
