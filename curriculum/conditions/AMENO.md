                                           Amenorrhea

Amenorrhea means not having menstrual periods.
Amenorrhea refers to the condition that in females who have never experienced menstruation until 18 years old (primary amenorrhea);  Or the menstrual cycle has been suspended for over 3 months in association with a number of other signs and symptoms(secondary amenorrhea).  Family history sometimes may be a clue.

Etiology & Pathogenesis

Blood stagnation

Blood exhaustion

Chong & Ren damage

The main cause of primary amenorrhea is late puberty.  In most cases this delay occurs for no known reason.  Sometimes it results from a hormonal problem, such as hypothyroidism, or genetic disorder, such as chromosome abnormalities.   In some cases, menstruation fails to occur because of a birth defect.  For example, a woman may not have a vagina or uterus.  Or the vagina may not have an opening that allows menstrual blood to escape.
The most common cause of secondary amenorrhea is pregnancy.  Usually a breast-feeding mother may not have menstrual periods.  Periods may also take 3 months or longer to resume after a woman stops taking birth control pills or stops nursing.
Secondary amenorrhea may also result from:
Emotional stress, brain injury, tumor in the pituitary gland,ovary or adrenal gland,or a cyst in the ovary, depression, pseudocyesis, thyroid problems, malnutrition, vigorous exercise, increased production of the hormone prolactin by the pituitary gland, drugs such as tranquilizers and antidepressants, rapid weight gain or loss, chemotherapy, chronic diseases such as kidney failure, cystic fibrosis and colitis, radiotherapy especially in the pelvic area, Asherman's syndrome (which is scarring of the lining resulting from an infection of surgery such as a D&C, heavy smoking, and etc.
Long lapses between periods, lasting 6 months or longer, are common with ongoing physical stress.  This is particularly the case if patients have lost a lot of weight, as with anorexia.  It may also happen if a patient has little or no body fat, as is true for some women athletes.
Permanent secondary amenorrhea occurs after menopause.  Menopause may occur prematurely before age 40.  Periods also stop after a hysterectomy.

Other symptoms depend on what is causing the amenorrhea.  For example, if a patient has a hormone imbalance, the patient may have a lot of body and facial hair, acne, breast milk secretions, a change in voice or sex drive, weight gain, or weight loss.

It is important to maintain a healthy lifestyle to prevent amenorrhea:

1.  Make changes in diet or activities to maintain the ideal weight.
2.  Avoid excessive use of alcohol and mood-altering stimulants or sedative drugs.
3.  Avoid cigarette smoking.
4.  Think about the area of emotional stress and conflict in life.  If the patient feels that she cannot resolve these conflicts on her own, ask for help from family, friends, or health professionals.
5.  Be moderate in all your activities.  Try to balance between work, rest and recreation.
6.  Maintain a positive outlook.
