Impotence

Erectile dysfunction (ED), a medical term for Impotence, is the male's sexual dysfunction characterized by the persistently inability to develop or maintain an erection of the penis satisfactory during sexual performance.

Etiology & pathology
In Western Medicine
Psychogenic cause: This is usually associated with sexual arousal or sexual attraction. These are about 10-20%, this type has better prognosis.

Disease cause: The common causative diseases are
Hypertension, atherosclerosis, DM, smoking, diet causes atherosclerosis, cholesterol deposits in the blood vessels, surgical removal of the prostate gland, operations in the pelvis, injuries to the penis, pelvis, spinal cord and brain, radiation therapy to the pelvis, the lack or excess of hormones, neurological disorders, side-effects from medications.

Treatments:
In Western Medicine: The physician will evaluate the condition in order to determine the specific cause of erectile dysfunction. The evaluation usually includes a medical history, a detailed sexual history, a physical exam and other basic blood tests. And then the physician will determine if the causes are psychological or physical.
If the cause is by severe clinical depression, antidepressant drugs are prescribed. If ED is caused by lack of hormones, hormone replacement therapy is used. If it is caused by psychological reasons, counseling or sex therapy may be recommended.
