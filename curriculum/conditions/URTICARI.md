                                                   URTICARIA

Urticaria is a commonly allergic skin disorder.  It is an eruption of skin characterized by temporary, flat-topped wheals which look like mealses or are as broad beans.  It is apt to appear after exposure to wind, so it is named  " wind wheal "  in TCM.  Because it comes and goes, it is also named " hidden rash ".
Another term for urticaria is hives.

Etiology and Pathogenesis

1.  Damp in the skin and muscle, attacked by wind heat or wind cold
2.  Accumulated heat in St and intestines, attacked by wind
3.  Intestinal parasites; or allergic to see food, such as fish, shrimp or crab

Western medicine takes urticaria as an allergic reaction to an irritant such as food, medicine, an insect bite or sting, infection, or emotiomal stress.  Histamine, a chemical the body makes, is released in response to the irritant that causes the hives to form . Histamine causes the redness, swelling, and itching.

Differentiation

Abrupt onset with itchy, raised, red/light red wheals that may vary in size and shape, or with pimples rising one after another.  The hives may appear on any part of the body with one or many areas.  They are most common on the arms, legs, and trunk.  It might be aggravated or alleviated by weather change.  The rash may last for a few minutes or several days.  Acute conditions subside quickly.
In the case of a severe reaction-to a bee sting, for example, the face and throat may swell.  Rarely, hives may cause problems with breathing, creating the danger of a severe asthma attack or a closing of the throat from swelling.
It is divided into the following types according to clinical manifestations.

1.  wind heat
     Red rashes with severe itching, floating and rapid pulse.
2.  wind damp
     White or light red rashes accompanied by heaviness of body, floating and slow pulse,         white and sticky tongue coating.
3.  accumulation of heat in the St and intestines
     Red rashes accompanied by abdominal or epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhea, thin    yellow tongue coating, rapid pulse.

Diagnosis

Find out the history of sensitivity to such things as:
Food (especially shellfish, eggs, milk, nuts, berries, dyes or other additives)
Plants (such as nettles) and pollens
Medicines (such as penicillin, aspirin, or sulfa drugs)
Animal fur
Insect bites or stings
Exposure to heat, cold, or sunshine.

It is easiest to identify drugs, foods, or plants that may cause hives because the response usually occurs within an hour.  Identify triggers such as emotional stress or multiple allergies may take more time and require skin tests or other types of tests.

We may suggest the patient to:
Keep a detailed diary of everything  the patient eats, drink, take, or are exposed to for 2-4 weeks.
Avoid foods, one at a time, to which we think the patient may be allergic.
See an allergist for skin tests.

Treatment

1.  avoid heat or rubbing, which releases more histamines.
2.  take antihistamine medicine to reduce the allergic response.
3.  for severe cases, take an oral steroid medicine ( e.g. prednisone).
4.  for emergent allergic reactions which cause hives spread quickly, a shot of antihistamine such as Benadryl is suggested.  If there is any suspicion that the throat is swelling or the patient is having trouble breathing or are wheezing, a shot of epinephrine (adrenaline) is advisable to counteract the reaction.
5.  in the case of life threatening, call 911 or refer to an ER.
