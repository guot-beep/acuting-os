                                          Morning  Sickness

Morning sickness is marked by a group of symptoms including nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dizziness during early pregnancy ( often within the first trimester of gestation ).  The severe cases are called hyperemesis gravidarum.  It is a common complaint in early stage of pregnancy and usually subsided between week 12 to 16.   Severe cases may emaciate the pregnant women and lead to other disorders.

Western Prospective

Uncontrollable nausea and vomiting during pregnancy that results in dehydration and acidosis is known as hyperemesis gravidarum, which is distinct from ordinary morning sickness with nausea and vomiting.  Many pregnant women with morning sickness feels as though they are vomiting everything they ingest, but if they continue to gain weight and are not dehydrated, they do not have hyperemesis gravidarum.  Persistent hyperemesis gravidarum is uncommon but may be associated with serious liver damage.

Treatment

Acidosis and dehydration are corrected with IV infusion of water, glucose, and electrolytes.  The patient should be kept in bed in a hospital and given nothing by mouth for 24 hours.  Antiemetics and sedatives should be used as necessary.  Occasionally, IV vitamin therapy is required.
