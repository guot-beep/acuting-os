Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

Multiple sclerosis is an autoimmune disease that affects the central nervous system. MS was first described in 1868 by Jean-Martin Charcot. Multiple sclerosis (MS) affects women more than men. The disorder is most commonly diagnosed between ages 20 and 40, but can be seen at any age. About 400,000 people in the U.S. have multiple sclerosis, and about one million patients worldwide.

Etiology and Pathogenesis
In western medicine, the exact cause is unknown. The most common thought is a viral infection or gene defect  or from environmental factors.
In multiple sclerosis, inflammation causes the myelin to disappear. Consequently, the electrical impulses that travel along the nerves become slower. In addition, the nerves themselves are damaged.

In 1996 the United States National Multiple Sclerosis Society standardized four clinical courses:
1. Relapsing-Remitting (RR): is characterized by unpredictable relapses followed by periods of months to years of remission. Deficits suffered during attacks may either resolve or leave sequelae.

2. Secondary progressive: More than half the patients who start with RR will turn into progressive. If the attacks are getting worse and closer they eventually become just one attack with no relapse.

3. Primary Progressive: 10-25% neurological function that continues steadily down. No stairs.

4. Progressive relapsing: 5-10% a sustained neurological relief with intense attacks. No steady period of stability and they end up in a wheel chair very quickly.

Genetic factors: the incidence of first-degree relatives of patients with multiple sclerosis if 3-5%; the incidence of the dizygotic the twins (dizytic twins) is 3-5%; and the incidence of the identical twins (monozytic twins) is 25%.

Diet component: Saturated fat and dairy is a major component.

Clinical Manifestation
In Western Medicine: Symptoms vary, because the location and severity of each attack can be different. Episodes can last for days, weeks, or months. These episodes alternate with periods of reduced or no symptoms (remissions).
Fever, hot baths, sun exposure, and stress can trigger or worsen attacks.
It is common for the disease to return (relapse). However, the disease may continue to get worse without periods of remission.
Patients with multiple sclerosis can have symptoms in many parts of the body.
If the damage is to the nerve fibers of the brain stem and cerebellum, double vision, difficulty swallowing, gait disturbance, dizziness, symptoms may occur. 如果脊髓发炎、除了运动、感觉功能外、排泄的功能亦发生障碍。
If the inflammation is on the spinal cord, in addition to the motor and sensory function, the function of excretion occurs. 少数病患还会有癫痫及智能障碍的发生。
