Glaucoma

Glaucoma is an eye disease in which the flow of aqueous humor (a clear, watery fluid normally circulates in the eye) is out of balance, with more fluid stay in than exiting due to the flow of this fluid out of the eye becomes blocked.  This results in a painful buildup of fluid in the eyeball that creates pressure, leading to impaired vision.  Patients often report seeing halos around lights in earlier stages.  If untreated, the increased pressure can cause atrophy of the optic nerve and result in blindness.
TCM refers to glaucoma as qing feng nei zhang, or "green wind internal blockage."

Etiology & Pathogenesis

Onset can be rapid and acute or slow and chronic.
There are two main types of glaucoma, depending on how the flow of fluid is blocked:
A.  Open-angle glaucoma - is caused by a gradual blockage in the area that drains fluid from the eyes into the drainage channels.  It is the more common type of glaucoma in the US and Europe.  It usually affects both eyes.
B.  Closed-angle glaucoma - is a blockage caused by a change in the position of the iris (the colored part of the eye).  The change in position causes the iris to block the drainage channels.  This type of glaucoma usually happens in one eye at a time.  If one eye occurs glaucoma, the other eye is at risk for having the same problem.  When this type of glaucoma happens suddenly, it is called acute closed-angle glaucoma and is a medical emergency.

Differentiation

Normal intraocular pressure (IOP) ranges between 11 and 21 mm Hg.  Some people with normal  pressure develop optic nerve damage (normal- or low-pressure glaucoma).  In contrast, many patients have pressure > 21 mm Hg without any optic nerve injury (ocular hypertension).
Open-angle and other chronic forms of glaucoma often have no symptoms in the early stages.  In later stages, patients will begin to notice a loss of vision (side vision is affected first).
Symptoms of acute closed-angle glaucoma include:
Severe eye pain;
Seeing halos around lights;
Nausea and vomiting;
Headache;
Loss of vision

Western Diagnosis

A comprehensive ophthalmic examination is essential for diagnosis and prompt treatment.  Examination includes visualization of the angle by a special prism or contact lens, IOP measurement, visual field examination and , most importantly, examination of the optic disks.  The eye doctor may use the following tests:
Tonometry - a painless procedure used to measure the pressure in the eye
Ophthalmoscopy - in which the doctor uses an instrument to look at the optic nerve inside
                               the eye
Visual Field Testing - which can show early changes in side vision caused by damage to
                                     the optic nerve
Gonioscopy - which is a method of examining the drainage channels

Weatern Treatment

The goal in the treatment of glaucoma is to reduce IOP.  This may be done with eyedrops, oral medication, laser surgery, or other types of surgery.  Some eyedrops reduce the amount of fluid made by the eye.  Others increase the amount of fluid that flows out.  Surgery relieves pressure inside the eye by opening up the drainage channel or by making another opening through which the fluid can drain.  Patients may need to take medication to control IOP for the rest of the life.
