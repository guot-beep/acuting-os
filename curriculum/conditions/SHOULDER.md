Frozen  Shoulder

The frozen shoulder also known as " 50-year-old shoulder " ( ??? ) in TCM because it usually occurs in people around their 50s.
In Western medicine, frozen shoulder is similar to periarthritis of the shoulder joint.

Etiology & Pathogenesis

Kidney xu ---- degeneration
Exhausted  ---- attacked by wind cold damp ----- Qi & blood stagnation at the shoulder
Injury to the tissures around the shoulder joint
Asleep ---- Qi xu
Lack of exercise

Differrnciation

Initial stage, mild soreness or pain (usually is negligible) on one side of the shoulder with movements.  Gradually, the pain becomes pronounced and even worse on exertion; results in limited range of motion at the diseased shoulder.
The  shoulder pain is aggravated progressively, spreading to the nearby tissures, up to the neck, down to the upper arm, and back to the nape and scapular area.  However, there is no redness, heat and swelling in the affected area.
Pain around the shoulder, worse at night, or woken up with pain.  Pain alleviated by exercise during the day.  Limited movement (in all directions) of the affected shoulder in severe cases does not allow patients to get dressed and undressed, or comb their hair.  Tender points around the shoulder (LI 15...)could be palpated.
Atrophy of the deltoid muscle on the diseased shoulder is present in chronic cases.

3 characteristics:
At initial stage, it is more painful;
At late stage, it is more limitation (ROM) due to adhesion around the shoulder;
At last, the affected shoulder and arm appears atrophy due to lack of or no motion.

6.  Exercise
     abduction, adduction, internal rotation, external rotation
External rotation - passive stretch. Stand in a doorway and bend your affected arm's elbow to 90 degrees to reach the doorjamb. Keep your hand in place and rotate your body as shown in the illustration. Hold for 30 seconds. Relax and repeat.

Forward flexion - supine position. Lie on your back with your legs straight. Use your unaffected arm to lift your affected arm overhead until you feel a gentle stretch. Hold for 15 seconds and slowly lower to start position. Relax and repeat.

Crossover arm stretch. Gently pull one arm across your chest just below your chin as far as possible without causing pain. Hold for 30 seconds. Relax and repeat.

Western View
X-rays. Dense structures, such as bone, show up clearly on x-rays. X-rays may show other problems in your shoulder, such as arthritis.
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and ultrasound. These studies can create better images of soft tissues. They are not required to diagnose frozen shoulder, however, they may help to identify other problems in your shoulder, such as a torn rotator cuff.
