DEVIATION  OF  EYE  AND  MOUTH

Deviation of the eye and mouth is derived from invasion of the meridians and collaterals and tendon muscle meridians( TMM ) in the facial region by exogeneous pathogenic wind - cold or wind - heat.  It can occur in patients of any age in any time, but mostly at the age of 20 - 40 years-old, in spring and fall, and more frequently in females.  Bell's palsy is similar to this disorder.  In Chinese it is known as kou yan wai xie.
In western medicine, it remains unknown cause.

Etiology and Pathogenesis

Facial meridians xu or vacancy, Plus
Invasion of wind cold /heat ------- qi & blood stagnation ------- malnutrition of Yangming and Shaoyang TMM ------- failure to function ------ paralysis of facial muscles & sinews ------- deviation of eye and mouth
Western medicine presumably thinks that Bell's palsy involves swelling of the nerve due to immune or viral disease, with ischemia and compression of the facial nerve in the narrow confines of its course through the temporal bone.

Differentiation

Usually, sudden onset, right after waking up ( pain behind the ear may precede facial weakness. Weakness develops within hours, sometimes to complete paralysis ), feeling stiff, heavy, numb, flaccid, but no sensory loss is demonstrable.  Incomplete closure of the eye in the affected side (palpebral fissure widens), drooping of the angle of the mouth, flattened nasolabial sulcus,  salivation.   Inability to frown, to raise the eyebrow, to close the eye, to blow out the cheek, to show the teeth or whistle.  The affected side becomes flat and expressionless, but sometimes patients may complain instead about the seemingly twisted intact side.  In some cases pain in the mastoid region or headache.

Deviation of eye and mouth to the healthy side, and in some cases disappearance of taste sense in front two thirds part ( A proximal lesion may affect salivation, taste, and lacrimation and may cause hyperacusis ).  In case of this condition prolonged, the deviation of mouth to the affected side might happen because of muscle contractures of the affected side facial muscles, which is called " inverted image ".
Usually there are two types:

Wind-Cold:  Onset with wind-cold invasion to the face, deviation of the mouth and eye,
                      Thin white tongue coating, floating tense pulse

Wind-Heat:  Onset secondary to flu with fever and chills, otitis media, tooth ache or gum
                    Inflammation, may present with headaches,  ache or pain in the ear and
                    mastoid area,  or sore throat
                    Thin yellow tongue coating, floating rapid pulse

Differential Diagnosis:  Weakness of the entire half of the face distinguishes Bell's palsy
                                      From supranuclear lesions (eg, stroke, cerebral tumor), in which
                                      The weakness is partial, affecting the musculus frontalis and
                                      Orbicularis oculi less than the muscles in the lower part of the
                                      Face.

Treatment

1.  Principle:  Eliminate wind cold/heat and remove the obstruction of meridians by applying even technique mainly to the points of Yang ming and Shao yang meridians.  At
the initial stage, mild stimulation and shallow insertion are suggested;   About 1 week later stronger stimulation or running thecnique can be applied;   About 10 days later electroacupuncture might be used.   In case of prolonged cases, warming needle and moxibustion are suggested, and the points in healthy side can be selected.
