                                       Insufficient  Lactation

Insufficient lactation refers to the common clinical symptom that milk secretion of a nursing mother is insufficient to feed the baby.  In some cases there may even be no
milk secretion at all.

Etiology and Pathogenesis

Constitutional spleen and stomach deficiency
Profuse loss of blood
Mental depression

Differentiation

1.   Qi and Blood Deficiency ( xu )

2.   Liver Qi Stagnation ( shi )

      Acupoints are mainly selected from St and Lr channels.

      shi type.

Western Prospect

Prolactin, which is secreted by the anterior pituitary gland in response to suckling, is responsible for milk production in the alveolus in the breast.  Stress such as  pain and anxiety can inhibit the let-down reflex.  The sight or cry of an infant can stimulate the release of oxytocin (a hormone secreted by the posterior pituitary upon the stimulation of the hypothalamus, is carried via the bloodstream to the breast and uterus, where oxytocin stimulates myoepithelial cells in the breast to contract and eject milk from the alveolus.) but not prolactin.
