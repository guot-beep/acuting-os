Conjunctivitis

Conjunctivitis is inflammation of the conjunctiva. ( The conjunctiva is the clear membrane that lines the eyelids and covers the white of the eye.)  It is sometimes called pink eye.   In TCM, Conjunctivitis is one of the common acute condition caused by wind heat invasion or liver and GB fire.  It is one of common seen  external eye disorders.  Overindulgence in alcohol, overuse of eyes, exposure to wind heat, lack of  light, and inadequate sleep may all contribute to making the conjunctivitis chronic.  It is known as   " red eye ", or " fire eye " in TCM.

Etiology and Pathogenesis

Conjunctivitis can be caused by many things, including infections by viruses or bacteria.  Viruses that cause colds may lead to conjunctivitis.  Some bacteria that cause conjunctivitis are chlamydia, staphylococci, and streptococci.
Viral and bacteria forms of conjunctivitis can be spread easily from person to person.  They can be spread by coughing or sneezing.  Bacteria or viruses can get in the eyes through contact with contaminated objects, including:  Hands, Washcloths or towels, Cosmetics, False eyelashes, Soft contact lenses.

Differentiation

Sudden onset.
Congestion, swelling and pain of eyelids, photophobia, lacrimation and  watery or sticky eye  discharge.
Wind heat:  itchy or scratchy eyes, redness
Liver & GB fire:  pronounced redness, discharge of pus, mood swings

Viral conjunctivitis usually gets worse 5 to 7 days after the onset.  It can improve in 10 days to 1 month.  If only one eye is affected at first, it may take up to 2 weeks for the other eye to be affected.  Usually, if both eyes are affected, the first eye has worse conjunctivitis than the second.
Bacterial conjunctivitis should improve within 2 to 3 days after  application of antibiotics.

Western Treatment

Viral conjunctivitis:  Antihistamine pills may be used to relieve the itching and redness.  Eyedrops may alos help control symptoms.

Bacterial conjunctivitis:  Antibiotic eyedrops.

It can also help the eyes get better by washing them gently to remove any pus or crusts, then dry them gently with a clean towel.

Patients who wear contact lenses need to stop wearing them until eyes are healed.

How to prevent conjunctivitis?
Wash hands frequently.  Do not touch or rub the eyes.
Never share eye makeup or cosmetics with anyone.
Never use eye medicine that has been prescribed for someone else.
Do not share towels, washcloths, or sheets with anyone.
Avoid swimming in swimming pools if you have conjunctivis.
