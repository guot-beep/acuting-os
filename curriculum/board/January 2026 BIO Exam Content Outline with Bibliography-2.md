## p.1

Biomedicine Content Outline
Effective September 1, 2026

Note to Candidate: This document serves as a guide to assist in examination preparation for
candidates who have met NCBAHMTM eligibility requirements. Below is the content outline for
the Biomedicine examination.

Please note: In regards to Clean Needle Technique (CNT), the Biomedicine module focuses on
universal precautions and emergency situations in comparison to the Acupuncture with Point
Location module which focuses on actual needling and its emergencies (e.g., needle angle and
depth).

Domain I. Biomedical Sciences (18% of Exam)
    A. Anatomy, physiology, and pathophysiology (6%)
         1. Identify anatomical structures and functions of body systems from a biomedical
              perspective.
         2. Differentiate between normal and abnormal structures and functions of body
              systems from a biomedical perspective.
         3. Recognize signs, symptoms, and co-morbidities associated with common medical
              conditions.1
    B. Pharmacology (7%)
         1. Identify common pharmaceutical categories and their functions.2
         2. Recognize interactions, side effects and adverse effects of pharmaceuticals.
    C. Supplements (5%)
         1. Identify commonly used supplements and their functions.3
         2. Recognize interactions, side effects and adverse effects of supplements.
         3. Identify clinically significant supplement � pharmaceutical interactions.

Domain II. Patient Evaluation (26% of Exam)
    A. Patient history (4%)
         1. Conduct a whole-person health interview (e.g., personal health history, social
              history, Activities of Daily Living (ADLs), functional assessment, mental health
              screening).
         2. Organize information obtained during interview into appropriate sections of the
              patient history.
    B. Physical examination (14%)
         1. Recognize different types of physical examinations (e.g., vital signs, orthopedic,
              neurological).
         2. Identify appropriate application of physical examination methods (e.g., observation,
              auscultation, palpation).
         3. Identify orthopedic examination techniques (e.g., range of motion, muscle strength
              testing, special tests, including orthopedic tests).
         4. Identify neurological examination techniques (e.g., assessment of cognitive
              function, evaluation of cranial nerves, sensory and motor function, dermatomal
              testing, deep tendon reflexes)
         5. Recognize how different types of physical examinations are performed.
         6. Interpret clinically significant findings from different types of examinations.
    C. Diagnostic tests (8%)
         1. Identify indications for common medical imaging (e.g., x-ray, MRI, CT, PET,
              colonoscopy, cystoscopy, bronchoscopy, ultrasound).
         2. Recognize the clinical significance of findings from medical imaging.
         3. Identify indications for common laboratory tests (e.g., complete blood count, basic
              metabolic panel, thyroid panel).
         4. Recognize the clinical significance of findings from laboratory tests.
         5. Identify indications for other diagnostic tests (e.g., EMG, EKG, biopsy).

Domain III. Analysis and Clinical Decision-making (34% of Exam)
         1. Analyze assessment findings to determine their relevance to presenting complaints.1
         2. Recognize red flags (e.g., signs of stroke, heart attack, suicidal ideation, domestic
              abuse, trauma).
         3. Establish and support the medical necessity for treatment.
         4. Establish case management options (e.g., treatment, referral, co-management,
              discharge).
         5. Differentiate between immediate, urgent, and routine referrals.
         6. Recognize the potential impact of social determinants/drivers of health on treatment
              outcomes.
         7. Recognize the potential impact of biomedical prognoses, management, and/or
              standard of care for common medical conditions on treatment outcomes.
         8. Comprehend and utilize evidence-based information in clinical decision-making
              (e.g., research, expert opinion).

Domain IV. Professional Responsibilities (22% of Exam)
    A. Professional and ethical conduct (5%)
         1. Comply with applicable federal laws and regulations (e.g., FDA, ADA, HIPAA, CDC).
         2. Comply with the legal scope of practice for Acupuncturists.
         3. Adhere to the NCBAHMTM Code of Ethics, Grounds for Professional Discipline, and the
              Procedures for Upholding Professional Conduct.
         4. Respect professional boundaries between patients and clinicians.
         5. Recognize and respond appropriately to unsafe or unprofessional practice.
         6. Treat all patients with empathy, dignity, and respect.
         7. Demonstrate respect for understanding non-discriminatory practices.
         8. Identify and manage potential conflict of interest situations.

    B. Infection control and workplace safety (3%)
         1. Identify modes of transmission of communicable diseases (e.g., airborne, fecal-oral).

         1. Document the following information in patient's chart: assessment findings,

              treatment plan, responses to treatment and plan for follow-up care.
         2. Maintain patient records in accordance with professional and legal standards.
         3. Maintain patient's privacy and confidentiality of protected health information in

              accordance with HIPAA and other legislation.
         4. Report instances of suspected abuse and neglect as required by law.
         5. Report instances of communicable disease to public health department.
    D. Insurance and billing (2%)
         1. Recognize the purpose and types of professional insurance (e.g., general liability,

              malpractice).
         2. Differentiate between ICD and CPT medical coding systems.
         3. Describe how basic ICD and CPT (e.g., E/M) codes are used in practice.
    E. Communication and collaboration with patients, clinicians, and the public (4%)
         1. Communicate clearly and be responsive to the needs of patients, clinicians, and the

              public.
         2. Obtain informed consent for treatment from patients as required.
         3. Work collaboratively with other clinicians in the best interests of the patient.
         4. Communicate the commonalities and differences between acupuncture and

              biomedicine (e.g., correlation of diagnostic categories, differences in uses of organ
              systems).

Biomedicine Examination Suggested Bibliography

The Content Outline is the primary resource for studying for this examination. The purpose of this
Bibliography is only to provide the candidate with suggested resources to utilize in preparation for the
examination. Candidates should feel free to consider other resources that cover the material in the
Content Outline.

There is no single text recommended by NCBAHMTM. All NCBAHMTM examination modules reflect
practice in the United States as determined by the most recent job analysis.

NCBAHMTM's item writers and examination development committee members frequently use the
following texts as resources; however, the sources used are not limited to the books listed here. The
NCBAHMTM does not endorse any third-party study/preparation guide.
