Foundations of Oriental Medicine Content Outline
Effective September 1, 2026

Note to Candidate: This document serves as a guide to assist in examination preparation for
candidates who have met NCBAHMTM eligibility requirements. Below is the content outline for
the Foundations of Oriental Medicine examination.

Domain I. Patient Assessment Methods (28% of Exam)
    A. Observation / Looking (Wang) (6%)
         1. Identify the manifestation of Spirit (Shen) (e.g., complexion, general behavior).
         2. Assess the appearance of the eyes, nose, ears, mouth, lips, throat, face (e.g., color,
              moisture, texture, corresponding organ locations).
         3. Identify manifestations of the tongue (e.g., body, coating, sublingual veins).
         4. Assess the appearance of the body (e.g., shape, movement, head, hair, neck, back,
              chest, abdomen, extremities, blood vessels, skin, nails).
         5. Assess the appearance of excretions (e.g., mucus, saliva, sweat, discharge, stool,
              urine, blood).
    B. Auscultation and Olfaction / Listening and Smelling (Wen) (3%)
         1. Assess respiratory sounds, voice, tonal qualities, and speech.
         2. Identify odors of the body, breath, excretions, secretions (e.g., urine, stool, vaginal
              discharge, sweat, wound exudates).
     C. Inquiry / Asking (Wen) (10%)
         1. Identify presenting complaints (e.g., onset, location, aggravation).
         2. Formulate additional questions based on patient's responses and examination
              findings.
         3. Conduct a Review of Systems (ROS) utilizing "Ten Questions" (Shi Wen) (e.g., pain,
              energy level, sweating, body sensations) and other approaches.
         4. Identify etiological factors, including internal (e.g., emotions), external (e.g.,
              environment) and miscellaneous (e.g., diet, occupation) factors.

    D. Palpation / Touching (Qie) (9%)
         1. Differentiate characteristics of the radial pulses (e.g., rate, depth, strength).
         2. Identify findings from palpation of other body areas (e.g., abdomen, torso, limbs)
              and channels (e.g., temperature, texture, pain, tissue quality, edema).

Domain II. Pathomechanisms and Pattern Differentiation (38% of Exam)
         1. Analyze assessment findings to establish pattern differentiation and underlying
              pathomechanism(s) based on the following theories:
              a. Yin/Yang
              b. Five Elements (Wu Xing)
              c. Eight Principles (Ba Gang) (e.g., interior, exterior, heat, cold)
              d. Qi, Blood, Body Fluids (Qi, Xue, Jin Ye)
              e. Channel (Jing Luo) (e.g., 12 primary channels, eight extraordinary channels, Luo-
                   connecting channels)
              f. Organ (Zang Fu)
              g. Six Stages (e.g., Tai Yang, Yang Ming, Jue Yin)
              h. Four Levels (Wei, Qi, Ying, Xue)
              i. Triple Burner (San Jiao)
              j. Internal etiology (e.g., seven emotions, stress)
              k. External etiology (e.g., wind, damp, cold, dryness, heat, summer heat,
                   pestilential Qi (Li Qi))
              l. Miscellaneous etiology (e.g., diet, lifestyle, trauma)
              m. Chinese organ clock

Foundations of Oriental Medicine Examination Suggested Bibliography

The Content Outline is the primary resource for studying for this examination. The purpose of this
Bibliography is only to provide the candidate with suggested resources to utilize in preparation for the
examination. Candidates should feel free to consider other resources that cover the material in the
Content Outline.

There is no single text recommended by NCBAHMTM. All NCBAHMTM examination modules reflect
practice in the United States as determined by the most recent job analysis.

NCBAHMTM's item writers and examination development committee members frequently use the
following texts as resources; however, the sources used are not limited to the books listed here. The
NCBAHMTM does not endorse any third-party study/preparation guide.

Cheng, Xinnong. (Ed.). Chinese Acupuncture and Moxibustion. 4th ed. Beijing: Foreign
    Languages Press, 2019.

Deng, Tietao. Practical Diagnosis in Traditional Chinese Medicine. London: Churchill Livingstone,
    1999.

Kastner, Joerg. Chinese Nutritional Therapy: Dietetics in Traditional Chinese Medicine. 3rd ed.
    New York: Thieme, 2021.

Maciocia, Giovanni. Diagnosis in Chinese Medicine: A Comprehensive Guide. 2nd ed. Philadelphia:
    Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2018.

---. The Foundations of Chinese Medicine: A Comprehensive Text for Acupuncturists and Herbalists.
    3rd ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2015.

---. The Practice of Chinese Medicine: The Treatment of Diseases with Acupuncture and Chinese Herbs.
    3rd ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2021.

Maclean, William, Jane Lyttleton, Mark Bayley, and Kathryn Taylor. Clinical Handbook of Internal
    Medicine, The Treatment of Disease with Traditional Chinese Medicine. 2nd ed. Seattle:
    Eastland Press, 2018.

Scheid, Volker, Dan Bensky, Andrew Ellis, and Randall Barolet. Chinese Herbal Medicine: Formulas
    and Strategies. Portable 2nd ed. Seattle, WA: Eastland Press, 2015.

Wiseman, Nigel, and Andy Ellis. Fundamentals of Chinese Medicine. Revised Edition. Brookline,
    MA: Paradigm Publications, 1995.

Wu, Yan, and Warren Fischer. Practical Therapeutics of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Ed. Jake P.
    Fratkin. Brookline, MA: Paradigm Publications, 1997.
