## p.2

FUNCTIONS OF
     YUAN SOURCE POINTS

          • Source point is related to the respective organ, channel, and original Qi
          • Each of the 12 meridians has a yuan source point

                • But what is Yuan Source Qi:

                        • Originates below the umbilicus and between the two kidneys, is dispersed to the Zang
                           Fu organs and further to the limbs via the San Jiao (CAM). It is life giving and the root of
                           the 12 channels. The san jiao causes the original yuan qi to differentiate for its different
                           uses around the body (to the organs and meridians)

Deadman, Peter, Mazin Al-Khafaji, Keven Baker. A Manual of Acupuncture. 2nd ed. East Sussex, England: Journal of Chinese Medicine Publications, 2007. Page 40

FUNCTIONS OF
     YUAN SOURCE POINTS

                • Yin Yuan Source points tonify Yin Organs

                        • Overlap with Shu stream Points – 3rd point from the distal end of the channel

                • Yang Yuan Source points reduce excess pathogenic factors in Yang Organs

                        • Except SJ 4, also relates to the original Qi
                        • Follows the Shu stream point – 4th point from the distal end of the channel (Except GB

                           which is the 5th point.

                • It is common to see differences within the same point category between zang
                  (yin) and fu (yang) points.

## p.6

FUNCTIONS OF
 LUO CONNECTING POINTS

        Deadman:
        1. Treating disorders of interiorly-exteriorly related channel or Zang Fu

              - Can use yuan source and luo connecting point pairs (Guest-Host)
        2. Departs from the main channel from the Luo connecting point and travels along its own trajectory

              - Treats disorders in regions reached by the Luo Connecting Channel
              - Increases area of influence of the main channel
        3. Treating psycho-emotional disorders

        Maciocia:
        1. In conjunction with Source point of the primarily affected channel
        2. According to their specific action regardless of their classification

              - Can either be FULL or EMPTY, just like the main channel (Ling Shu chapter 10)

Deadman, Peter, Mazin Al-Khafaji, Keven Baker. A Manual of Acupuncture. 2nd ed. East Sussex, England: Journal of Chinese Medicine Publications, 2007. Page 40

## p.8

LUO CONNECTING CHANNELS

             • The connecting channels are called luo mai: luo implies the meaning of “network”
             • The Main Channels are called Jing Mai and jing implies the meaning of “line, route or

                way” (Maciocia)

The Foundations of Chinese Medicine: A Comprehensive Text for Acupuncturists and Herbalists. 3rd ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2015. Page 848-850

## p.9

LOCATION OF LUO CONNECTING
       CHANNELS

             • Three separate entities
                  • Luo Connecting Channels themselves

                           • Qi and Ying Level

                  • Space more superficial than the Luo connecting channels (all the way to the skin)

                           • Minute/superficial collaterals (Sun Luo/Fu Luo)
                           • Wei Level – protecting and warming

                  • Between primary channels and the internal organs

                           • Connect to blood vessels and Xue (Blood) level
                           • Nourishing

             • Luo connecting channels are prone to Qi and Blood stagnation

## p.11

GU EST-HOST COMBO:
    YUAN SOURCE + LUO CONNECTING

             • When tonifying a specific channel or organ using the source point, the Luo connecting
                point of the interiorly-exteriorly associated channel may be used to strengthen the
                treatment. (Deadman)
                   • Enforce the Yuan Source Point of the affected channel or organ due to its
                     interior/exterior or Yin/Yang relationship.

             • Also called the Guest-Host method
                   • Host = Yuan Source, where the pathology lies
                   • Guest = the interior/exterior paired channel

The Foundations of Chinese Medicine: A Comprehensive Text for Acupuncturists and Herbalists. 3rd ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2015. Page 850

## p.12

GU EST-HOST COMBO CONT

             • CAM suggests a slightly different use of Guest Host
                   • When a disease occurs in both an interior and exterior channel (ex: lung and Large
                     Intestine) needle the yuan source of the channel that was first affected and the luo
                     connecting point of the channel that was affected second
                   • Ex: LU9 + LI6 or LI4 + LU7

             • The symptoms of each Luo Connecting channel often include ones from its respective
                interiorly/exteriorly related channel.

The Foundations of Chinese Medicine: A Comprehensive Text for Acupuncturists and Herbalists. 3rd ed. Philadelphia: Elsevier Churchill Livingstone, 2015. Page 850
