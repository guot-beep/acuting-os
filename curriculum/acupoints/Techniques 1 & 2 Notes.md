Gua Sha
Definition
Gua = scraping/rubbing
Sha = “sandy diseases”
Refers to different things:
Acute illnesses [sunstroke, gastritis, cholera, convulsions, etc.]
Petechiae or reddish, elevated millet-like skin rash
Unknown virus or bacteria inhaled or enters through skin
Functions
Opens orifices
Detoxes Heat & fire poison
Clears exterior condition
Balance Yin & Yang
Manipulation
Use porcelain spoon or Gua Sha tool with oil
Scrape along DU, UB, ST meridians, elbow, shoulder, wrist, popliteal fossa until skin gets red or dark red
Directions
Chest: Medial  Lateral
Posterior aspect of body:  Superior  Inferior [start at base of neck]
Abdomen:  Superior  Inferior
Limbs:  Proximal  Distal
Color:  Petechiae should fade within 2-4 days.  If it takes longer to fade, can indicate poor blood circulation
Very Light:  Blood Deficiency
Fresh Red:  Recent penetration of Sha
Dark Red:  Heat
Purple/Black:  Long term blood stasis
Indications:  Acute Excess Diseases
Wind Cold:  GB 20, UB 11, LU 1
Wind Heat:  Along UB meridian
Acute gastroenteritis:  REN 4 & 6, ST 25, ST 34, ST 36, ST 37, SP 9
Headache:  GB 20, GB 40, ST 8, Tai Yang, LI 4, LU 7
Sunstroke:  DU 26, LI 11, PC 3, PC 8, KD 1
Acute Sore throat:  Along DU channel on upper back, LU channel from elbow to thumb
Chronic Sore Throat:  REN channel on upper chest
Cough & Phlegm:  Upper chest, Upper back along DU channel
Precautions/Contraindications
Do not apply to broken or ulcerated skin, lumps
Abdominal & sacral regions of a pregnant woman
Caution:  Patients susceptible to spontaneous bleeding, low platelet counts, too weak or too nervous

Different needling reactions [De Qi]
Distention & soreness:  Indicates good outcome.  Often seen with deficiency syndromes or constitutions.
Heaviness:  Feeling of not being able to move a part of body because it feels heavy.  Common with LI 4.
Tingling:  Up/down channel or at point.  Good indication that channel stagnation has been shifted or moved.
Numbness/Electric Shock Feeling:  Stronger reaction.  Can be helpful in excess syndromes, acute pathologies & patients with strong constitutions.  If patient reports a point or larger area feels numb, can have several causes  Reassure patient & do Tui Na after removing needles.  Possible causes:  Needling too strongly, Needling into nerve tissue, Underlying blood deficiency
Heat Sensation:  Desirable response when patient suffers from cold syndrome (cold, deficiency, arthritis, diarrhea due to cold-damp, low back pain due to kidney deficiency, facial paralysis, muscle atrophy)
Cold Sensation:  Positive response in treatment of heat syndromes (Wind Heat invasion, dysmenorrhea or migraines due to Liver Fire or Blood Heat, Stomach Fire, etc.)
Sharp pain not intended or acceptable for vast majority of points, except Jing Well points and some others in thin-skinned, thin muscled areas on hands, feet & ear.
	Insertion angles:
Perpendicular:  90 degrees
Most common angle for insertion
Used where muscles are thick/dense & no danger of internal organ damage.
Oblique:  45 degrees
Used where subcutaneous flesh is thin or internal organ lies beneath point.
Commonly used on chest & back.
Transverse or horizontal:  10-25 degrees
Used in areas of little flesh or very thin muscles.  Used for points on head, face & some chest points.
Also an appropriate angle for running needling to join points or for threading techniques.

Fundamental Manipulation Techniques
Thrusting & Lifting:  Excess Syndrome, Strong Constitution
Twirling or Rotation:  Stimulates Qi response
Strong:  Large arc (360 degrees), Rapid frequency
Mild:  Lesser arc (180 degrees), Slower frequency
Do not rotate needle in just one direction in a large arc because it can damage tissue or lead to stuck needle
Tube Insertion Methods
Squeezing, Nail Flicking, One Tap, Two Taps, Flutter
