Entry Exit Points
◦ As the energy circulates through the 12 major meridians, it "exits" one meridian and "enters" 
into the following meridian.  This can be a common area for qi to get “blocked”.
◦ Not all of the entry exit points are the first and the last points on a meridian. 
o The exceptions are LU 7 , LI 4, ST 42, KD 22, PC 8, TH 22 and GB 41.
◦ They ma y be considered if you want to allow a relative excess or deficiency in one 
meridian to be offset by it's yin yang paired meridian or between meridians that connect. 
For example, if you want to drain excess from the LU and ha ve it flow into a deficient LI 
you could needle LU 7 and LI 4.
◦ Blocks are most often seen between meridians which connect, as opposed to yin/yang 
pairs such as L V/GB or ST /SP . 
◦ The Entry Exit points are used often in Five Element (Worsley) Style Acupuncture

Mirroring Concept / Cross-Needling
◦ Instead of needling on the affected area unilaterally or bilaterally to gain 
greater effect, needle is inserted on the opposite side as if a mirror divides the 
body in half. 
◦ Examples: Elbow for knee; Front for back; right for left
◦ Note: Generally speaking, points are selected unilaterally from the affected side of the body, or else 
needled bilaterally, However , since the channels run bilaterally it is not uncommon to select points 
from one side of the body to treat disorders on the opposite side (Deadman).

◦ Chronic Hemiplegia (greater than 3-6 months)
◦ Reducing the healthy side 
◦ Reinforcing the affected side 
◦ When affected side is too painful or swollen to needle, like an acute ankle sprain.
◦ Reducing the healthy side 
◦ When you want to mobilize the affected side to promote Qi and Blood flow and 
remove obstructions that may be causing pain.
◦ Healthy side needled while moving the diseased side

Contralateral needling
◦ Indication: acute painful disorder 
◦ This utilizes the Six Channel/Division Relationship 
◦ Select points from the ankle to treat the contralateral wrist, and vice versa
◦ Select points from the knee to treat the contralateral elbow , and vice versa
◦ Select points from the hip to the treat the contralateral shoulder, and vice versa
◦ Select points from the palm to treat the contralateral sole, and vice versa
◦ Select points from the fingers to treat the contralateral toes, and vice versa

Chronic Cases or Channel Issues
◦ Contralateral distal points (in this case, distal means away from the area 
affected) are often used to treat acute cases.
◦ In chronic problems, local and distal points are used to reinforce each other’ s 
action on the channel.
◦ Chronic issues may be beyond simple MSK sprains and strains, for instance, you may 
use ST 44 + LI 4 (distal points) to treat toothache, gum issues, and facial pain on the 
opposite side in addition to some local points on the affected side, like ST 5, ST 3 due to 
the channel trajectories
