## `supp.vitamin_d3` — Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) / 維生素 D3
- category: `vitamins`
- common_forms: cholecalciferol (D3) softgel/capsule, drops/liquid, combined calcium+D
- typical_dose_range: Adults: DRI 600–800 IU/day (15–20 mcg); common supplement products often provide ~1,000–2,000 IU/day; adult UL 4,000 IU/day unless medically supervised.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Excess intake can cause hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria.
  - Thiazide diuretics can increase hypercalcemia risk; corticosteroids and some weight-loss/cholesterol drugs can alter vitamin D status/absorption.
  - No specific anticoagulant, immunosuppressant, or thyroid-drug interaction is a defining D3 interaction, but medication review remains appropriate.
- evidence_snapshot: Established nutrient for calcium/bone metabolism; supplementation benefit is strongest for preventing/treating deficiency rather than as a universal disease treatment.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.b_complex` — B-complex vitamins / 維生素 B 群
- category: `multi_ingredient`
- common_forms: tablet/capsule blends, high-potency B-complex, B-complex with vitamin C
- typical_dose_range: No standardized formula. Usually provides multiple B vitamins from around Daily Value levels to high multiples; assess each component separately, especially B6, niacin, folic acid, and B12.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Safety depends on individual component doses; high-dose B6 can cause neuropathy and niacin can cause flushing/hepatotoxicity at pharmacologic doses.
  - Folic acid can mask hematologic signs of B12 deficiency.
  - Review interactions component-by-component; no single class-wide anticoagulant/immunosuppressant/thyroid interaction rule.
- evidence_snapshot: Useful when one or more B-vitamin needs are not met, but a B-complex is not one standardized intervention and evidence cannot be generalized across formulations.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/MVMS-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.b12` — Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) / 維生素 B12
- category: `vitamins`
- common_forms: cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, sublingual/tablet, injection (prescription/clinical)
- typical_dose_range: Adult RDA 2.4 mcg/day; oral supplements commonly contain much higher amounts (e.g. 25–1,000+ mcg) because absorption is limited and dose-dependent.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Generally low toxicity; no established UL.
  - Metformin and acid-suppressing drugs can reduce B12 status over time.
  - No characteristic anticoagulant/immunosuppressant/thyroid-drug interaction.
- evidence_snapshot: Strong evidence for correcting B12 deficiency; benefit in people with adequate status for nonspecific energy/cognition claims is not established.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.folate` — Folate / folic acid (vitamin B9) / 葉酸 / 維生素 B9
- category: `vitamins`
- common_forms: folic acid, 5-MTHF/methylfolate, multivitamin/prenatal formulations
- typical_dose_range: Adults DFE RDA 400 mcg/day; pregnancy 600 mcg DFE/day. Common supplements often provide 400–800 mcg folic acid or equivalent; adult UL for folic acid from supplements/fortified foods is 1,000 mcg/day.
- key_safety_notes:
  - High folic acid intake can mask hematologic signs of vitamin B12 deficiency.
  - Interacts with some antifolate drugs and antiseizure medicines; medication-specific review required.
  - No general anticoagulant or thyroid-drug warning.
- evidence_snapshot: Established role in one-carbon metabolism and prevention of neural tube defects when adequate folic acid is taken per public-health guidance around conception.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.coq10` — Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) / 輔酶 Q10
- category: `antioxidants_coenzymes`
- common_forms: ubiquinone, ubiquinol, softgel
- typical_dose_range: Common clinical-study/supplement range ~100–300 mg/day; higher doses have been studied for specific conditions. No U.S. RDA.
- key_safety_notes:
  - May interact with warfarin and potentially reduce anticoagulant effect; monitor if combined.
  - May interact with insulin/diabetes treatment.
  - Compatibility with some cancer treatments is uncertain; coordinate with oncology team.
- evidence_snapshot: Evidence is condition-specific and mixed; NCCIH does not support broad disease-treatment claims, though limited signals exist in selected cardiovascular/migraine contexts.
- sources:
  - https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10

## `supp.ginkgo` — Ginkgo biloba extract / 銀杏葉萃取物
- category: `botanical_extracts`
- common_forms: standardized leaf extract tablets/capsules, liquid extract
- typical_dose_range: Common research dosing ~120–240 mg/day standardized extract, divided or once/twice daily; product standardization matters.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Potential bleeding risk/interaction concern with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs; perioperative review warranted.
  - Seeds are toxic when raw/roasted in quantity; supplement data mainly concern leaf extract.
  - No established thyroid-drug benefit; medication review required.
- evidence_snapshot: NCCIH reports no conclusive evidence that ginkgo is helpful for any health condition; dementia prevention and tinnitus benefit have not been demonstrated.
- sources:
  - https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginkgo

## `supp.curcumin` — Curcumin / turmeric extract / 薑黃素
- category: `botanical_extracts`
- common_forms: turmeric root powder, standardized curcuminoid extract, enhanced-bioavailability curcumin with piperine/lipid systems
- typical_dose_range: Research products vary widely, commonly hundreds of mg to ~1–2 g/day curcuminoids/turmeric extracts; formulations are not dose-equivalent because bioavailability differs.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Highly bioavailable formulations have been associated with liver injury; stop and evaluate if jaundice/dark urine or marked systemic symptoms occur.
  - Potential antiplatelet/bleeding interaction is plausible; use caution with anticoagulants/antiplatelets.
  - Piperine-containing products can alter drug exposure; interaction review is important.
- evidence_snapshot: NCCIH considers evidence insufficient for definitive health-purpose conclusions; osteoarthritis results are promising but not definitive.
- sources:
  - https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric

## `supp.lutein` — Lutein / 葉黃素
- category: `antioxidants_coenzymes`
- common_forms: lutein softgel, lutein + zeaxanthin, AREDS2-style eye-health combinations
- typical_dose_range: Common eye-health formulations/studies often use lutein 10 mg/day with zeaxanthin 2 mg/day; no RDA established.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Generally well tolerated in studied doses; high carotenoid intake can cause benign skin discoloration.
  - No defining anticoagulant/immunosuppressant/thyroid-drug interaction established.
  - Combination products may introduce other interaction risks.
- evidence_snapshot: Lutein/zeaxanthin are retinal carotenoids; evidence is strongest in specific ophthalmic contexts and combination formulations rather than as a general-purpose supplement.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/list-all/

## `supp.multivitamin` — Multivitamin/mineral / 綜合維生素礦物質
- category: `multi_ingredient`
- common_forms: once-daily MVM, age/sex-specific formulas, prenatal formulas
- typical_dose_range: No standard formulation or dose; many products provide nutrients near Daily Values, while high-potency products may greatly exceed them.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Interaction and toxicity profile depends on ingredients and dose; duplicate intake with other supplements is a major issue.
  - Vitamin K-containing products can affect warfarin management.
  - Minerals such as calcium/iron can interfere with levothyroxine absorption if taken too close together.
- evidence_snapshot: MVMs can help fill nutrient gaps, but compositions vary widely and broad prevention benefits are not uniform across outcomes.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/MVMS-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.calcium` — Calcium / 鈣
- category: `minerals`
- common_forms: calcium carbonate, calcium citrate, calcium + vitamin D
- typical_dose_range: Adult RDA generally 1,000–1,200 mg/day total intake depending on age/sex. Supplemental doses are often 500–600 mg per dose; total intake should account for diet. Adult UL commonly 2,000–2,500 mg/day depending on age.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Higher supplemental intake can increase kidney-stone risk in some people.
  - Calcium can reduce absorption of levothyroxine; separate administration per medication guidance.
  - Also interacts with some antibiotics and bisphosphonates; no defining anticoagulant interaction.
- evidence_snapshot: Essential for bone and neuromuscular physiology; supplementation is most rational when dietary intake is inadequate or specific clinical indications exist.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.magnesium` — Magnesium / 鎂
- category: `minerals`
- common_forms: magnesium citrate, magnesium glycinate, magnesium oxide, magnesium chloride
- typical_dose_range: Adult RDA from all sources roughly 310–420 mg/day by age/sex. Supplemental products often provide ~100–350 mg elemental magnesium/day; U.S. UL for supplemental magnesium is 350 mg/day, excluding food magnesium.
- key_safety_notes:
  - High supplemental doses commonly cause diarrhea; extreme doses can cause serious toxicity, especially with renal impairment.
  - Can reduce absorption of some antibiotics and bisphosphonates; separate dosing.
  - No defining anticoagulant or thyroid interaction, but product timing with medications should be reviewed.
- evidence_snapshot: Essential mineral with established deficiency indications; evidence for symptom-specific supplementation varies by condition and baseline status.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.zinc` — Zinc / 鋅
- category: `minerals`
- common_forms: zinc gluconate, zinc acetate, zinc picolinate, zinc sulfate
- typical_dose_range: Adult RDA 8 mg/day women and 11 mg/day men; common supplements often 10–30 mg/day. Adult UL 40 mg/day.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Chronic high doses can cause copper deficiency and neurologic/hematologic problems.
  - Interacts with quinolone/tetracycline antibiotics and penicillamine; separate dosing.
  - No defining anticoagulant or thyroid-drug interaction.
- evidence_snapshot: Established nutrient with clear deficiency treatment value. Benefit beyond correcting inadequate zinc status is indication-specific.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.creatine` — Creatine / 肌酸
- category: `amino_acids_performance`
- common_forms: creatine monohydrate powder, capsules, buffered/other salts
- typical_dose_range: Well-studied regimen: loading ~20 g/day divided for 5–7 days, then maintenance ~3–5 g/day; loading is optional.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Typical doses are generally well tolerated in healthy adults; weight gain from water retention and GI effects can occur.
  - People with kidney disease or complex medical conditions should use clinician guidance.
  - No characteristic anticoagulant/immunosuppressant/thyroid-drug interaction established.
- evidence_snapshot: One of the best-supported sports supplements for repeated high-intensity exercise, strength and power; little value for endurance performance.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.nad_plus` — NAD+ / NAD+（菸醯胺腺嘌呤二核苷酸）
- category: `antioxidants_coenzymes`
- common_forms: oral NAD+ capsules/tablets, sublingual products, IV/IM products marketed outside standard supplement evidence base
- typical_dose_range: No established evidence-based oral dose or RDA. Commercial dosing varies widely; direct oral/IV NAD+ evidence is much less mature than NR/NMN precursor research.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Long-term safety and clinically meaningful oral bioavailability are uncertain.
  - Interaction data with anticoagulants, immunosuppressants, and thyroid drugs are insufficient rather than proven absent.
  - Parenteral NAD+ should not be treated as equivalent to ordinary dietary-supplement use.
- evidence_snapshot: Human evidence for direct NAD+ supplementation is limited; avoid extrapolating benefits observed with NR/NMN or preclinical NAD biology to direct NAD+ products.
- sources:
  - https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements

## `supp.nr` — Nicotinamide riboside (NR) / 菸醯胺核糖（NR）
- category: `antioxidants_coenzymes`
- common_forms: nicotinamide riboside chloride capsules, NR combination products
- typical_dose_range: Human trials commonly study ~100–1,000 mg/day; one randomized trial found 100–1,000 mg/day well tolerated over 8 weeks. No RDA established.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Short-term human safety is reasonably characterized at studied doses, but long-term clinical outcome data remain limited.
  - Interaction data with anticoagulants/immunosuppressants/thyroid drugs are insufficient.
  - Do not infer anti-aging or disease-treatment benefit from NAD+ biomarker increases alone.
- evidence_snapshot: NR can raise NAD-related biomarkers in humans, but clinically meaningful benefits for aging or disease outcomes remain unproven/condition-specific.
- sources:
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6611812/
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684646/

## `supp.nmn` — Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) / 菸醯胺單核苷酸（NMN）
- category: `antioxidants_coenzymes`
- common_forms: NMN capsules/tablets, powder/sublingual marketed forms
- typical_dose_range: Small human trials have commonly studied roughly 250–1,200 mg/day for weeks to a few months; no established RDA or consensus therapeutic dose.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Short-term trial safety appears generally acceptable, but long-term safety and efficacy remain uncertain.
  - Interaction data with anticoagulants/immunosuppressants/thyroid drugs are insufficient.
  - Regulatory status and product quality should be checked because marketplace status may change.
- evidence_snapshot: Early human trials suggest NMN can affect NAD-related biomarkers, but clinical benefits remain preliminary and should be labeled uncertain.
- sources:
  - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=nicotinamide+mononucleotide+randomized+trial+human
  - https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements

## `supp.omega_3` — Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA/ALA) / Omega-3 脂肪酸
- category: `fatty_acids`
- common_forms: fish oil EPA+DHA, algal DHA/EPA, krill oil, ALA plant oils
- typical_dose_range: ALA AI: ~1.1–1.6 g/day for adults. EPA+DHA supplement amounts vary; many products provide ~250–1,000 mg/day combined EPA+DHA, while prescription-level triglyceride treatment uses higher doses under medical supervision.
- key_safety_notes:
  - High-dose omega-3 can increase bleeding-time laboratory effects; clinically significant bleeding risk is usually low but anticoagulant/antiplatelet users need review, especially at high doses.
  - High-dose omega-3 may increase atrial fibrillation risk in some cardiovascular trials.
  - No characteristic thyroid-drug or immunosuppressant interaction.
- evidence_snapshot: EPA/DHA lower triglycerides at pharmacologic doses; cardiovascular outcome effects depend on formulation, dose, and population, so fish-oil products are not interchangeable.
- sources:
  - https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/

## `supp.probiotics` — Probiotics / 益生菌
- category: `probiotics`
- common_forms: Lactobacillus-containing products, Bifidobacterium-containing products, multi-strain capsules/powders, fermented foods
- typical_dose_range: No universal dose. Products commonly contain millions to tens of billions of CFU per serving; efficacy is strain-, indication-, and product-specific.
- key_safety_notes:
  - Usually few side effects in healthy people, but serious infections have occurred in vulnerable populations; FDA/NCCIH highlight particular concern in premature infants.
  - Use caution in severely immunocompromised or critically ill patients; this is the key immunosuppression-related safety flag.
  - Drug interaction data are not captured by a single class-wide rule.
- evidence_snapshot: Some specific formulations show benefit for selected GI/other indications, but effects cannot be generalized across all probiotic strains/products.
- sources:
  - https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/probiotics-usefulness-and-safety

## Global caution
Dose ranges are descriptive reference/study/common-product ranges, not prescribing instructions. Multi-ingredient products require component-level interaction review. `unknown/insufficient interaction data` must not be converted to `no interaction`.
